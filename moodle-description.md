# Moodle description draft (under 500 words)

**Domain, why and who.** This visualisation explores how Victorians commute to work, focusing on public transport use, car dependence and working from home. The topic matters because commuting affects time, household cost, access to jobs, congestion and quality of life. The intended audience is the general Australian public, especially Victorian residents and students who understand everyday travel but may not know how strongly commuting behaviour changes by location.

**What.** The visualisation uses public Australian Bureau of Statistics data from the Census of Population and Housing, ABS QuickStats and Community Profiles, and the ABS article *Australia's Journey to Work*. These sources provide travel-to-work mode shares for Victoria, selected local areas, Australian states and national time trends from 2011 to 2021. Geographic information is used to place selected Victorian areas on a simplified Victoria map.

**How.** The page is structured as a scrollable story rather than an expert dashboard. It begins with headline statistics, then compares the main 2021 travel modes, shows geographic differences, explains the rise of working from home, and finishes with car dependence, public transport decline and active transport. Different Vega-Lite idioms are used for different tasks: a donut chart summarises composition, a heatmap compares areas and modes, grouped bars support local comparison, a bubble map shows spatial patterns, ranked bars reveal high and low public transport areas, a scatter plot shows the inverse relationship between car and public transport use, line and area charts show change over time, a dumbbell plot compares capital cities with the rest of each state, and stacked bars show household vehicle ownership.

The main design goal is clarity for non-expert readers. Colour is used consistently: red for car travel, blue for public transport, green for working from home, and orange/purple for active travel. Annotations, section text and source notes guide the reader through the argument: Victoria is not one commuting system. Inner Melbourne has a mixed-mode pattern, outer and regional areas remain much more car-dependent, and working from home changed commuting demand across the whole state in 2021.
