How Victorians Commute

Public transport halved in the pandemic Census, car-as-driver fell to a 40-year low, and working from home jumped from 5% to one in four Victorians in a single Census cycle.

Storyline

Three forces explain Victoria's 2021 commute.

The data tells a layered story. Place is the first force: inner Melbourne behaves like a dense, mixed-mode European-style city while outer suburbs and regional centres are car-dependent. The pandemic is the second: a single Census cycle drove working from home from 4.6% to 25.7% in Victoria, halved public-transport use and pushed driving to a 40-year low. The long run is the third: active transport peaked in 2011 and even working from home, the headline 2021 story, sits on top of a 50-year baseline that hovered around 5%. The next fifteen views unpack each force in turn.

Chapter 3 · Public transport and car dependence

The other half of the story: where cars and trains lost ground.

The pandemic Census also exposed the structural geography of car dependence. Capital cities are systematically less car-driven than the rest of each state — except in the Northern Territory, where the pattern inverts because nearly all jobs are in Darwin. Train, bus and tram commuting fell across the board in 2021, but train use led the collapse with a 61% national fall.

Chapter 4 · The long view

Active transport's slow retreat.

Conclusion

Victoria is not one commuting system.

Three answers fall out of the data. Place still defines mode: Melbourne City uses public transport at 14× the regional rate. The pandemic compressed two decades of work-from-home growth into one Census cycle and pulled public transport back to where it was in the 1990s. The long run tells us active transport has been declining since 2011 even before COVID — so the post-pandemic recovery question is not just about restoring PT, but rebuilding walk-and-bike infrastructure that has been quietly losing ground for a decade. Whether the 2021 break holds or fades is what the 2026 Census will tell us.

Methodology & definitions

Every figure on this page traces to a public ABS source. "Car as driver" follows the ABS strict definition (employed people aged 15+ whose main method of travel was driving a car). At LGA level the only published time-series is the id.com.au "private vehicle" composite (driver + passenger + truck + motorbike), which is used in charts 02, 03, 04, 05 with that wording; this composite typically runs 2–4 percentage points above strict car-as-driver. WFH and public-transport definitions follow the ABS "Method of Travel to Work — 15 modes" classification (MTW15P). Method of travel relates to Census day (Tuesday 10 August 2021) only, so the 2021 figures should not be read as a long-term baseline — they are heavily affected by stay-at-home orders in NSW and VIC. The pre-2016 active-transport per-mode split in chart 14 is estimated from ABS Census historical summaries because the line-item split was not consistently published before 2016; totals are exact from the ABS Active transport users table.
