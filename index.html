How Victorians Commute

Public transport collapsed in the pandemic Census, car travel remained dominant, and working from home rewrote the weekday travel story.

54.5%used a car to get to work in Victoria in 2021
25.7%worked from home in Victoria in 2021
4.4%used public transport in Victoria in 2021
-61%national train commuter decline, 2016 to 2021
Why

Commuting shapes time, cost, access and quality of life. The project explains why transport choices look very different across Victoria.

Who

The audience is the general Australian public, especially Victorian residents and students interested in cities and everyday travel.

What

ABS Census and QuickStats data are combined with geographic boundary information and area centroids.

How

Fourteen Vega-Lite views use map, heatmap, ranked bar, grouped bar, scatter, line, dumbbell, donut and stacked area idioms.

Storyline

Three forces explain the 2021 commuting picture.

First, cars remain the default outside the inner city. Second, public transport use is strongly geographic: it is highest where density, train access and CBD proximity overlap. Third, work from home changed the denominator itself, reducing the number of people making any commute on Census day.

Conclusion

Victoria is not one commuting system.

Inner Melbourne behaves like a dense, mixed-mode city; outer suburbs and regional centres remain car-dependent; and the 2021 work-from-home shock reshaped demand everywhere. The key insight is not only that public transport fell during COVID-19, but that recovery will be uneven unless access outside the inner city improves.
